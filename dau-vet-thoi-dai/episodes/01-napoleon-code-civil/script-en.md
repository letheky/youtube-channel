# Did Napoleon Write the Napoleonic Code?

DRAFT · NOT VOICE LOCKED

## S01 — One Name. Two Questions.

A legal code bears Napoleon's name. But did the ruler personally write its provisions? And does giving a country a common code mean giving everyone equal rights? Those questions take us from the drafting room to a rule about marriage.

## S02 — A Collective Project

In 1800, Bonaparte appointed four jurists to prepare a draft: Portalis, Tronchet, Maleville and Bigot de Préameneu. Courts were consulted, and the Council of State debated the text. Napoleon provided political drive. Treating him as its sole author would erase the work of its legal drafters.

Sources: E01, E02

## S03 — The Name Came Later

On 21 March 1804, thirty-six laws were brought together as the Civil Code of the French. It was renamed Code Napoléon in 1807. A ruler's name became attached to a major achievement, but a title is not a full list of its creators.

Sources: E01, E02

## S04 — What Was Unified?

France had regions governed by customary law and others drawing on written law influenced by Roman law. The code unified private law: rules concerning persons, property and civil relationships. That changed how law was organised. It did not, by itself, settle who enjoyed equal rights.

Sources: E03

## S05 — A Boundary Within the Family

Article 213 in the 1804 edition required a husband to protect his wife and a wife to obey her husband. The inequality was explicit. This concerns married women's position under a historical provision. It does not mean that every woman had the same legal status, or that this wording remains in force.

Sources: E04, E06

## S06 — An Inheritance That Changed

The version of Article 213 effective from January 1971 gives spouses joint responsibility for the family's moral and material direction. The article number survived; its substance changed. A lasting legal inheritance does not mean that every rule from 1804 survived unchanged.

Sources: E05

## S07 — A Legacy Includes Its Limits

Napoleon drove a collective project; he did not write the entire code himself. And a unified code could still contain unequal rights. Assessing his legacy means examining both the institutions he helped build and their limitations. Both belong in the same account.

Sources: E02, E03, E04, E05
